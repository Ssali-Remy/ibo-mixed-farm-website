Welcome to the farm

A family farm that became a school.

We started in 2004 with one idea: that a piece of land in Uganda can do far more than it is usually asked to do. Bananas, coffee, mangoes, vegetables, dairy cattle, goats, sheep and bees — all on roughly fifteen acres in Bwizibwera, all working together.

Farmers began arriving to ask how it was done. Then agricultural colleges. Then universities, NGOs and government programmes. Today IBO Mixed Farm Ltd is a registered company, an accredited skilling centre under the Uganda Vocational and Technical Assessment Board, and still — every single morning — a farm that has to produce.

That is the whole point. Nothing is taught here that we do not do ourselves, at commercial scale, with the same weather and the same markets you face.

The land

Fifteen acres, seven enterprises.

Every block on this farm earns its keep, and every block doubles as a classroom. This is what "optimum utilization of land" looks like when you write it down:

  • Bananas9.5 acres — 4,222 plants under cultivation
  • Coffee1.8 acres
  • Orchard1 acre of mangoes
  • Mother garden1 acre, CWD-resistant material
  • Horticulture1 acre — cabbage, tomatoes, green pepper, vegetables
  • Nursery1 acre — 100,000 plantlet capacity
  • Apiary0.2 acres — 86 hives

Livestock training takes place on the adjoining family holding — a herd of 120 cattle, 60 goats and 12 sheep, used for practicals.
